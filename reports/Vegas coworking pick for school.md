# Skip Cowork Lounge, anchor at StartUpNV downtown

**Top pick: the International Innovation Center @ Vegas (IIC) at 300 S. 4th St. #180 downtown.** The nonprofit StartUpNV has run it since May 2026. Its own pricing page lists an **unlimited hot desk at $150/month**, and it hosts free startup coworking Fridays ([StartUpNV IIC](https://startupnv.org/iic/); [Nevada Business Magazine](https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/05/startupnv-takes-over-management-of-international-innovation-center-in-downtown-las-vegas/)). It is the only space in the research that combines verified current operation, a community of founders, and an unlimited plan inside your $100–200 budget. It also sits a short drive from the Historic Westside, where the new West Las Vegas Library has free podcast and video studios and Black business institutions are concentrated. **Backup: a Regus coworking membership "from $119–129/month"** at 500 N. Rainbow or 2300 W. Sahara, about 10–12 minutes from you. These are quiet business centers with call-friendly space and little community, and the cheap tier is likely a limited-days plan ([Regus Las Vegas](https://www.regus.com/en/us/nevada/las-vegas/coworking); [CommercialSearch – Regus Sahara](https://www.commercialsearch.com/coworking-property/us/nv/las-vegas/nv-las-vegas-sahara/)). Your instinct about Cowork Lounge looks right. A Primerica insurance office lists the same unit, and the online footprint is thin. Even if it runs as coworking, its cheapest membership ($280) is over budget. One caveat applies to everything here: **no operator website could be opened directly in this research**. Prices and open/closed status come from search-result summaries of operator pages and from listing aggregators, which are often stale. Call before you pay.

## $150 buys unlimited desk time and a founder network at IIC

The IIC wins because it is backed by more than an aggregator listing. StartUpNV's takeover was covered by **Nevada Business Magazine and News 3 LV in May 2026**. Coverage says the center offers hot desks **24/7, by the day or month**, along with a business address, mail handling and a free entrepreneur resource library ([News 3 LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/startupnv-takes-over-las-vegas-international-innovation-center-expands-coworking-hub)). StartUpNV's own price sheet lists a **$150 hot desk, a $250 dedicated desk**, and conference rooms at $500 per half day ([StartUpNV IIC](https://startupnv.org/iic/)). A 24/7 hot desk matters for a student who takes online classes at night. No other sub-$200 option in the research offers verified after-hours access. Elsewhere, 24/7 access only comes with memberships of $280 and up.

The community fit is also strong for your AI consulting firm. StartUpNV is the state's startup accelerator. It runs **Free Co-Working Fridays** at the IIC ([Luma](https://luma.com/abk3xcuk)), so you'd be working near founders and investors rather than insurance agents. The research found **no Black-owned coworking space or recurring Black-tech meetup operating in Vegas in late 2026**. Downtown is the closest you can get. The IIC is near the Historic Westside, where the Urban Chamber of Commerce (1951 Stella Lake St.) has a new CEO, Christopher Shelton, as of Aug 2026 ([VegasInc](https://vegasinc.com/business/2026/aug/03/new-chamber-of-commerce-leader-emphasizes-access-f/)). The free West Las Vegas Library studios and the coming Innovation Commons are there too. One name can mislead: UNLV's "Black Fire Innovation" is a Caesars/UNLV hospitality-tech brand, not a Black-focused program ([UNLV](https://www.unlv.edu/news/article/unlv-black-fire-innovation-fueling-southern-nevadas-growing-tech-ecosystem)).

The costs are real. The drive is **about 20–25 minutes each way** (a map estimate, not routed), and **there is no free parking**, only metered street spots and a paid garage ([Luma](https://luma.com/abk3xcuk)). The garage rate was not found. If it runs $10 or more a day, three trips a week could add $120+ a month and push total cost past Regus. There are also conflicts to resolve. A directory lists the IIC "from $25/day" rather than monthly ([Vegas Cowork](https://www.lasvegascowork.com/)). Pacific Workplaces is listed at the **same 300 S. 4th St. building** with a $25 day pass and a "$49/mo" plan of unknown terms ([CommercialSearch](https://www.commercialsearch.com/coworking-property/us/nv/las-vegas/las-vegas-2/)). These may be the same floor under old and new branding, or two separate operators. The free Friday is described as every Friday in some places and only the first Friday in others ([Fabulous Nevada](https://fabulousnevada.com/community/nonprofit-of-the-month-startupnv/)).

## Regus near Rainbow is the quiet, close backup for calls

Regus is the backup because it is near you, and IWG is an established chain that keeps its centers listed as active. The **500 N. Rainbow** center is about 10 minutes away, and **2300 W. Sahara** is about 12. Regus advertises a citywide **coworking membership from $129/mo** and hot desks from $25/day, and one Sahara listing shows $119 ([Regus](https://www.regus.com/en/us/nevada/las-vegas/coworking); [CommercialSearch](https://www.commercialsearch.com/coworking-property/us/nv/las-vegas/nv-las-vegas-sahara/)). The same Regus page also quotes desks "from $219/mo." That signals the $129 tier is a limited-days, business-hours plan, possibly tied to a long contract, so ask about both before signing. Expect professional, quiet space suited to interviews, with little networking. Choose Regus over the IIC if a phone call shows that downtown parking pushes the IIC's real cost past about $200, or if most of your week is calls rather than community.

A third, closer option is **CoOperate at Black Fire** (8400 W. Sunset Rd., about 10–12 minutes away, (702) 900-0440). It sells day passes on LiquidSpace and shares a building with the free Nevada SBDC advisors ([LiquidSpace](https://liquidspace.com/us/nv/las-vegas/co-operate-black-fire-coworking-space/day-pass); [UNLV SBDC](https://www.unlv.edu/sbdc/services)). Its day-pass price was not captured, and it is open weekdays roughly 8–5 only ([DayPassCoworking](https://daypasscoworking.com/listing/cooperate-at-sunset-las-vegas)). It works as an occasional stop on days you're meeting an SBDC advisor, not as a home base.

## A weekly setup that uses free library studios for recording

No single sub-$200 space covers recording, so split the week by task. **Online classes and deep work:** spend two or three days at the IIC, including Friday for the coworking crowd and late-night sessions if 24/7 access holds. On the other days use **Spring Valley Library** (Mon–Thu 10–8, Fri–Sun 10–6, (702) 507-3820), or a Spring Mountain Rd cafe like Broken Coffee for low-stakes studying ([LVCCLD Spring Valley](https://thelibrarydistrict.org/locations/SV/); [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/search?find_desc=Laptop+Friendly+Cafe+Free+Wifi&find_loc=Spring+Valley%2C+NV)). **Interviews and client calls:** book a free Spring Valley Library study room near home. The April 2026 rules cap bookings at **2 hours per day with no stacking**, which is fine for single calls ([LVCCLD guidelines](https://thelibrarydistrict.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/54/2026/05/Study-Room-Guidelines.pdf)). Use an IIC phone booth too, if the call confirms one exists. **Video and podcast recording:** the new **West Las Vegas Library** has podcast studios, video rooms and a green screen at no cost, after a free intro class ([LVCCLD](https://thelibrarydistrict.org/news/new-state-of-the-art-west-las-vegas-library-opens-its-doors/); [Fox5](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/03/04/west-las-vegas-library-offers-free-creative-tech-resources-community/?outputType=amp)). It is close to the IIC, so pair recording with downtown days. Enterprise Library also has a podcast space nearer the southwest ([LVCCLD](https://events.thelibrarydistrict.org/event/12588825)). **Networking:** go to StartUpNV Fridays, Tech Alley (free, third Saturdays, next on **Nov 21, 2026**), Urban Chamber events, and Black-owned-business listings on Eventbrite ([Tech Alley](https://www.meetup.com/tech-alley/); [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/d/nv--las-vegas/black-owned-business/)). AWS re:Invent (Nov 30–Dec 4) also brings AfroTech-listed events to town ([AfroTech](https://afrotech.com/black-tech-conferences-2026)).

Before going anywhere, call and ask these questions. The research did not turn up phone numbers for the IIC or the Regus centers, so use the contact details on [startupnv.org/iic](https://startupnv.org/iic/) and [regus.com](https://www.regus.com/en/us/nevada/las-vegas/coworking).

## Conclusion

Searching for a "coworking space" frames the problem too narrowly. In Vegas in late 2026, the best value at your budget is a mix: a nonprofit-run startup hub for desk time and community, plus the public library system for free private calls and studio-grade recording that no $150 membership includes. Listings conflict and spaces close often, as Cowork Lounge and The Coop show. The most reliable signal of a live space is recent institutional news coverage, not directory reviews. The IIC is the only budget option with that.

The thinnest part of this answer is Black professional community. Nothing dedicated exists in Vegas right now, and the institutions being built for it, the Historic Westside Innovation Commons and the MYS Firm training center, will not open before 2027 ([Las Vegas Black Image](https://lasvegasblackimage.com/2026/05/historic-westside-entrepreneurial-center-breaks-ground/)). Basing yourself downtown and joining the Urban Chamber now would put you in that network early, as an AI consultant already working in the area when those spaces open.
